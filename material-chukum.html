Natural Mayan plaster

Chukum

Millennial. Natural. Alive.

A natural plaster of lime and resin from the Havardia albicans tree, used by the Mayan civilization for millennia across the Yucatán Peninsula. A matte, faintly velvety surface in warm beige-rosé tones that vary naturally. Waterproof, breathable, antibacterial — with no synthetic additives.

The origin

A recipe thousands of years old

Chukum takes its name from the tree Havardia albicans, endemic to the Yucatán Peninsula. The Maya extracted resin from its bark and combined it with lime to obtain a finish resistant to the tropical climate — used on temples, cisterns and homes for thousands of years.

The technique was rediscovered in 1993 by Mexican architect Salvador Reyes Ríos, who preserved the original recipe intact. The resin gives the surface its warm, faintly rosy tint and a natural water resistance, with no synthetic polymers.

Application is entirely artisanal, in successive layers polished by hand, so that every surface remains unique and impossible to reproduce industrially.
